# PRODUCT.md

## 1. The job

When I’ve visited two shops and can’t remember or compare what the shopkeepers told me, I want help choosing a product that fits my needs and negotiating the price, so I can use it without regret.

Other moments it happens: At the first shop, I miss some questions and don’t remember every answer. At the second shop, I ask different questions and struggle to compare the information.

Who, by situation: A person buying a product offline who needs to compare offers from different shops.

Today they hire: Friends’ opinions, online reviews, AI guidance, and their own notes and memory.

## 2. The switch

What they’d fire: Relying on memory and incomplete notes to recall shopkeepers’ answers and compare offers mentally.

Push: They are busy and don’t have time for extensive product research.

Pull: They want a product that fits their needs at a better price.

Anxiety: The app might misunderstand shop information or use information that isn’t current.

Habit: They ask a friend and buy the product their friend bought to feel safe.

What the product does about each:

- Push → Prepares guidance and consistent questions based on their needs and budget.
- Pull → Compares products against their needs and provides bargaining guidance supported by collected offers.
- Anxiety → Keeps source labels visible, marks unknown information, and asks the buyer to confirm unclear details. Recent shop information is not automatically verified.
- Habit → Explains the comparison so the buyer can review it. Sharing with someone they trust is a nice-to-have feature.

The one worry onboarding must remove: “I’m afraid of choosing the wrong model or product.”

## 3. The core flow

Today (what they do now, before the product):

1. Work out what they need.
2. Set a budget.
3. Search online.
4. Read reviews and watch videos.
5. Call friends for advice.
6. Find nearby shops.
7. Visit the first shop and ask questions.
8. Try to remember its models, prices, and conditions, or save highlighted details in notes.
9. Visit another shop and ask questions.
10. Try to compare both offers.
11. Call or revisit shops for forgotten details.
12. Check fit, installation, warranty, and service.
13. Choose, bargain, and buy.
14. Keep the purchase details and final price.

With my product:

1. Enter the product, purpose, budget, and requirements. Choose to prepare for visits or compare existing offers.
2. If preparing, review relevant guidance and consistent questions. Add extra questions if needed. Buyers with existing offers skip this step.
3. If planning visits, provide a PIN code or location, select shops from Google Maps suggestions, and see a suggested visit order to reduce travel. Buyers with existing offers can skip this and add shops directly. Listings do not confirm stock or prices.
4. Visit shops, ask questions, and inspect products. Skip this when using offers already collected.
5. Upload voice notes, written notes, and product photos or screenshots under the correct shop. Show upload success or failure and allow retries without losing saved information.
6. Confirm the model, specifications, price, quote date, and included costs the app understood. Keep the original source attached. Mark unclear information as unknown and ask the buyer to check it. Label unsupported claims “Shopkeeper said; not verified.”
7. Compare different brands and models against needs and technical specifications. Confirm missing details that could change the recommendation, including total costs, fit, installation, warranty, local service, and availability. Keep sources and uncertainty visible. Different specifications or inclusions must not appear as equivalent offers. If no product fits, say so and continue searching.
8. Review bargaining guidance supported by comparable, current offers. Confirm current price and availability for old quotes or quotes without validity dates. Matching some specifications does not make prices equivalent. If evidence is insufficient, say so instead of inventing a bargaining price.
9. Choose, bargain, and buy after checking the product physically.
10. Confirm what was bought and the final price paid.

Repeat steps 4–6 for each shop. Important missing specifications must be checked before a final recommendation. The app must not guess missing information or treat buyer confirmation as independent verification of a shopkeeper’s claim.

Things it takes to get the job done today: About 14 actions · With my product: About 10 actions. These are working estimates, not measured evidence of less effort. Some actions repeat per shop; buyers with existing offers skip preparation. The intended improvement is less forgotten information, repeated checking, confusion, and unnecessary travel.

## 4. Onboarding

First value: Guidance and questions that prepare the buyer to collect comparable information. For buyers with existing offers, first value is a comparison with sources and missing details clearly marked.

The smallest commitment we ask for: What product they want, why they need it, and their budget. Buyers comparing existing offers also provide notes or photos.

The worry it removes: “I’m afraid of choosing the wrong model or product.”

From opening the app to first value:

1. See “Choose a product that fits your needs and budget” and choose “Prepare for shop visits” or “Compare existing offers.” Removes: Uncertainty about where to start.
2. Enter the product, purpose, and budget. Removes: Worry that guidance or comparison won’t fit their needs.
3. For preparation, receive relevant guidance and consistent questions, with the option to add questions. For existing offers, upload notes or photos, confirm captured details, and see the comparison. Removes: Worry about missing important information or being unable to compare offers.

Existing-offer buyers do not have to repeat education, select suggested shops, or plan a route.

Login: When saving the first shop’s information, after useful guidance where applicable.

What we don’t ask on day one: A profile, a tour, or phone permissions at the start.

What we ask later, and when: Ask for a PIN code or shopping location when the buyer chooses to plan visits. Request microphone or photo access only when the chosen capture method requires it.

Education focuses on relevant specifications and questions to ask. “Latest models” and approximate market prices are not onboarding promises. External product facts must have identifiable sources; unverified claims remain labelled throughout capture and comparison.

## 5. v1

Does: Helps buyers prepare consistent questions and add their own, select shops from Google Maps near their chosen location, see a suggested visit order, capture shop information, confirm unclear details, compare models against their needs, get evidence-supported bargaining guidance, and record their purchase. Buyers can also start with existing notes or photos.

Why these belong: Shop suggestions and visit-order planning help reduce travel. Voice capture reduces writing during visits. Comparison and supported bargaining guidance address the agreed job.

Doesn’t: Automatic shop calls, buying through the app, or help after purchase. Listings do not guarantee stock or prices; bargaining guidance does not guarantee a discount.

Nice to have: Sharing comparisons with friends or family, and comparison history for revisiting earlier purchases. Saving and reopening the current purchase is required in V1.

How I’ll know it worked: During the same purchase, beta users add another shop’s offer and use the updated comparison to narrow their choice or ask a specific follow-up question. I also collect feedback. Longer term, they return for a second purchase.

## 6. The riskiest guess

If this is false, the product is pointless: Buyers need help remembering and comparing shop offers, and will capture enough information during visits to get that help.

Evidence so far: I collected information on my own, took photos, and saved highlighted details in Google Notes. I asked both shops about latest models, warranty, and price. The shops offered different brands and models, and my notes weren’t detailed enough for a clear comparison.

Thirty-minute check, no code: Explain the process once and give one buyer a consistent question list before visits to two shops. Observe what they capture, how long capture takes after each visit, and how much prompting they need. After the visits, spend 30 minutes manually comparing the captured information, marking missing details and sources. Share the comparison and observe whether they narrow their choice or ask a specific follow-up question. Measure capture time separately from the 30 minutes of comparison work.

Starting limits, set before the test:

- Capture takes no more than three minutes after each shop to add voice notes and photos.
- Explain the process once before visits. The buyer does not need a personal reminder after each shop.
- Capture the model, quoted price, included costs, and answers to important questions. Unknown answers are acceptable if clearly marked.
- The buyer uses the comparison to narrow their choice or ask a specific follow-up question.

Count the test as failed if capture exceeds the time limit, requires repeated personal reminders, or leaves too little information to take either next step. Record actual capture time, reminders, missing details, and buyer actions. These are starting limits to test, not proven standards.

What happened: The structured test has not been run yet. The earlier installation-cost example was assumed, not observed.

## 7. Milestones

Each checkpoint has a result that can be demonstrated in about ten seconds. The manual buyer test stays in section 6.

1. I can add two shop offers and compare them. I enter needs and budget, upload existing notes or photos, confirm captured details, and see a comparison with sources and missing information marked.
2. I can add another shop. Its offer updates the comparison without losing earlier information.
3. I can prepare for shop visits. I receive guidance and consistent questions, and can add my own.
4. I can select shops and see a suggested visit order. Suggestions come from Google Maps near my chosen location.
5. I can get bargaining guidance. It uses comparable quotes and says when evidence is insufficient to suggest a price.
6. I can record my purchase. I save what I bought and what I paid.
7. I can reopen the app and find my saved information.
