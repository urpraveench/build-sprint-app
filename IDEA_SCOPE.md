IDEA LOCK · Build Sprint

The idea, in one line: A shopping companion app for users who prefer offline shopping, helping them visit multiple stores, compare items, and make informed purchasing decisions.

Why me: I have firsthand experience with this problem. I have navigated these challenges myself and frequently help friends when they are buying large appliances. This practical experience, combined with AI integration, positions me well to solve this user pain point.

GOAL
The one goal they hire it for: To save time and, ultimately, money.

Delta 4 (the steps today → the steps with my product):

* Today's Process: You go to one shop to ask for details, then visit another to compare. You might forget which questions to ask, fail to capture negotiation details, or struggle to compare specs from memory once you get home. It is a cumbersome and disorganized process.
* With the Product: The app asks what product the user wants to buy, curates relevant educational content, and highlights key factors to consider *before* shopping. It locks in a budget and clarifies the user's specific needs. Armed with this data, the user gets an optimized offline shopping experience. They know exactly which questions to ask, the right price range for negotiation, and how to choose the right product, eliminating the traditional hassle.

USER
The trigger (when the pain hits): When people need to buy high-consideration items, especially home appliances, that require visiting physical stores. Certain items shouldn't be bought blindly online; customers need to touch and feel the product to ensure it fits their needs.

Visiting multiple shops, however, exposes buyers to aggressive sales tactics where shopkeepers might try to bluff or push inventory that doesn't fit the user's true needs.

This app helps users avoid those traps by enabling informed decisions. It advises on what to look for, which questions to ask, and automatically compares products across neighborhood shops. Users simply upload shopkeeper conversations, product screenshots, or feature lists, and the AI processes this data to provide objective, hassle-free comparisons.

Today's path, step by step:

1. Conduct initial internet research on available products.
2. Watch YouTube review videos.
3. Read comparative articles.
4. Call friends or family to ask about their purchases (what they bought, cost, and from which shop).
5. Visit the shop to gather information, often forgetting to ask critical questions.
6. Fall victim to smooth sales tactics, resulting in buying the wrong product and experiencing buyer's remorse.

Who they trust on this decision: A friend or relative who recently purchased a similar product, and to a lesser extent, the shopkeeper.

Would they pay?: Yes, users would be willing to pay on a per-purchase basis. By using this offline comparison tool, users can save an estimated 10% on the product cost. For example, on an average purchase of ₹25,000, a 10% saving is ₹2,500. This clear ROI gives customers a strong incentive to pay for the service.

PRODUCT
Onboarding: The user experiences immediate value when the app curates targeted educational content based on the current market and generates a customized list of critical questions to ask the shopkeeper before their first store visit.

The core loop (User Stories):

* Story 1 (Pre-Shopping): The user inputs the product they want to buy. The AI app asks follow-up questions regarding budget, desired features, and the primary use case. Based on these inputs, the app provides tailored informational content (product varieties, current offers, key specs) and generates a definitive list of questions the user must ask when visiting the shop.
* Story 2 (In-Store & Comparison): The user visits the first shop, asks the AI-generated questions, and feeds the answers, product screenshots, and shop details (including contact numbers) back into the app. The app verifies if any critical questions were missed.
* Story 3 (Subsequent Visits & Decision): The user visits a second shop. The app provides basic questions plus new comparison questions based on data from the first shop (e.g., specific price negotiation points or verifying a feature shop 1 mentioned). The user logs the answers and screenshots. Once satisfied with the visits, the user asks the app to summarize. The AI provides a comprehensive breakdown of pros and cons between the shops, recommends the best option based on the user's initial budget and needs, and suggests a target negotiation price. The user can repeat this loop for as many shops as needed.

The AI-first part: AI drives the entire experience, functioning as the core engine for onboarding, continuous engagement, and the primary comparison/decision loop.

MARKET
Tailwinds: While e-commerce is a massive trend, a significant portion of consumers still demand the tactile experience of offline shopping for big-ticket items. We are blending the traditional human-centric offline experience with an intelligent digital AI agent to assist the buyer.
Competitors: Platforms like Buyhatke and KompareAI focus strictly on online price comparisons, while apps like ShopSavvy scan barcodes in-store for online price matching. None of them address the specific problem of navigating localized, conversational negotiations with physical shopkeepers.
Size and fit: My immediate addressable market includes an extended network of about 300 friends and family, with at least 20 people having purchased a major product this year.
